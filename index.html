Key findings

  • Capture guidance is mostly good. Six of eight guide the shot with a frame, mask or distance bar. Best documented: Anyline TireBuddy.
  • Failed scans are the biggest gap. Four of eight show no screen for a bad photo. None shows an automatic rejection after capture.
  • Uncertainty is almost never shown. Only TreadView gives a confidence figure (66%) and a range. No product publishes accuracy data.
  • Reports end at a shop, not a decision. Next steps are a store button, a quote or generic advice. No reminders in any of the eight.
  • Access runs from free to sales-gated. A free web page (Tire-kan), a paid app (TreadView), a licence key (Anyline), a dealer link (TyreSwift).
  • The starting sheet needs corrections. Anyline is now TireBuddy, TyreSwift runs CaRool's engine, and CaRool's prize was for hardware. See Reference.

Limits: desk research. No app was installed; only Tire-kan's web page was opened live, up to its terms checkbox. YouTube blocked video downloads, so frames come only from vendor-hosted videos. Some screens are old (Tire-kan 2019 to 2020, CaRool 2023). "Not observed" means not seen in public material, not absent.
